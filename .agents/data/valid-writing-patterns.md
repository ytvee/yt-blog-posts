# Valid Writing Patterns

Use this file as the positive style gate for drafting, revision, platform adaptation, and final QA.

These patterns are derived from `.agents/project/40-style-profile.md`, `.agents/templates/writing-style-guide.md`, `.agents/data/brand-profile.md`, and the saved style examples.

## Mandatory Positive Patterns

Every substantial draft or revision should include several of these patterns:

- Concrete start: begin from a real observation, work situation, number, mistake, practical tension, or sharp reframe.
- Personal-to-system movement: start close to experience, then show the system, incentive, business consequence, or operating lesson behind it.
- Authorial position: write as someone making a judgment, not as a neutral explainer summarizing the internet.
- Specific anchors: use dates, counts, budgets, durations, ratios, tools, constraints, or named situations when they sharpen credibility.
- Honest trade-off: show what becomes easier, harder, riskier, slower, cheaper, or more expensive.
- Useful skepticism: remove hype by naming the constraint, hidden cost, missing condition, or operational reality.
- Clear inference: connect facts to a practical conclusion without inflating the claim.
- Varied rhythm: mix short and medium paragraphs; use short sentences for emphasis, not as a constant dramatic device.
- Natural first person: use first-person voice when it reflects real experience, curiosity, doubt, or responsibility.
- Concrete ending: close with a sharpened observation, practical takeaway, or genuine question that follows from the post.

## Strong Structural Moves

Prefer these structures when they fit the topic:

- observation -> reframe -> analysis -> practical takeaway
- experience -> mistake -> numbers -> lesson
- curiosity -> exploration -> explanation -> invitation to respond
- framework -> numbered breakdown -> synthesis
- technical problem -> why it matters -> practical implementation path

Do not force a structure if the current post has a better natural shape.

## Sentence And Paragraph Rules

- Paragraphs should feel intentionally paced, not mechanically equal.
- A section may open with a scene, doubt, concrete claim, question, or dry observation.
- Avoid closing every paragraph with a polished summary line.
- Prefer exact plain wording over decorative phrasing.
- Use questions as genuine invitations or thinking prompts, not engagement bait.

## Positive Gate

Before returning prose, confirm:

- the opening could not be pasted into a generic business or AI article unchanged
- the text contains concrete evidence of lived or operational judgment
- the argument has at least one useful trade-off or constraint
- the rhythm is not uniformly smooth
- the ending lands on a real thought rather than a formula

If the draft fails this gate, revise it before final QA.
