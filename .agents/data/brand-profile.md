## Content Strategy

### Core Content Pillars (in order of importance):

6. Personal experience as developer and entrepreneur

8. Product launches and knowledge packaging
9. Engineering culture
10. Balance between professional growth and quality of life

### Content Philosophy:
**Not:** news-driven media, trending hype, mass case studies, clickbait
**Yes:** rare, interesting, useful ideas; evergreen essays; long-term relevant materials; original insights; personal experience; practical frameworks

### Content Flow:
English/personal source idea → rare or underestimated insight → personal experience → adaptation for CIS audience → practical conclusion → checklist, framework, or clear model

### Publication Frequency:
2 large evergreen essays per month (approximately 6-8 min reading time / 4,000-8,000 words each)

**Quality over frequency.** Consistency matters more than volume.

### Post Format:
- Evergreen essays (not news)
- Personal experience and case studies
- Practical frameworks and models
- Interviews and conversations (planned)
- Tutorials and how-tos (secondary)

## Style & Tone

**Overall Tone:** calm, personal, expert, practical, respectful to reader

**What the Style Is:**
- Personal and authentic
- Expert without arrogance
- Practical and actionable
- Systems-oriented
- Honest about failures and limitations
- Educational
- Conversational yet structured
- Observational

**What the Style Is NOT:**
- Motivational / toxic productivity preaching
- Hype-driven
- Generic or polished corporate speak
- Robotic AI transitions
- Inflated conclusions that say little
- Clickbait or sensationalism
- Overly formal or academic
- Trend-chasing

**Tone Characteristics (from examples):**
- Calm, not urgent
- Thoughtful and deliberate
- Curious and exploratory
- Sometimes critical of trends
- Honest about unknowns
- Asking questions to readers
- Using personal data and examples
- Long-form, deep thinking
- Anti-marketing, anti-pressure

**Rhythm & Pacing:**
- Medium sentence length, varied
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences often)
- Space for breathing and reflection
- Logical development of ideas
- Not rushed

**Common Structural Patterns (from examples):**
- Personal experience or observation as opening
- Specific examples with numbers/details
- System or pattern analysis
- Critical thinking about the subject
- Questions to audience
- Practical conclusion or framework
- Sometimes: lesson learned / what-next
