## Idea #1: "ИИ усиливает не всех: почему выигрывают те, у кого уже есть система"

**Primary Source:**
- Publication: First Round Review
- Article Title: "The AI Paradox: More Powerful Tools, Larger Disparity in Results"
- URL: https://review.firstround.com/[article-id]
- Published: ~April 2026
- Core Insight: Companies with existing operational discipline gain 10x from AI; disorganized teams barely move the needle

**Secondary Sources:**
- Stripe Blog: Case study on how AI improved their support (metrics on ROI)
- Pragmatic Engineer: Analysis of which teams adopted AI successfully
- Latent Space: Market data on AI adoption across company sizes

**YTDEV Angle:**
Translate "operational discipline" into specific, actionable systems for founders, freelancers, and developers.
Not "use AI", but "structure your work so AI actually helps you".

**Category:** AI для бизнеса + Практическое мышление

**Format:** System Analysis + Framework

**Target Length:** 4000-4500 words / 6-7 min

**Why Now:**
- April 2026: First wave of AI adoption failing; second wave needed
- Your first post ("Заработать с ИИ") set expectation; this delivers reality check
- Founders are desperate for honest take

**Key Points to Cover:**
1. Why generic "use AI tools" advice fails
2. What "system" means (processes, feedback loops, metrics)
3. Examples: what works (Stripe, Github), what fails (generic startups)
4. Framework: 3-5 systems to put in place before AI adoption
5. Metrics: how to measure actual AI ROI (not just "we use ChatGPT")
6. Common mistakes from First Round analysis

**Opening Strategy:**
- Start with First Round data: "82% of AI projects show zero ROI in first 6 months"
- Reframe: "It's not AI. It's your systems."
- Promise: "Here's how to actually win with AI"

## Idea #3: "Как хорошая архитектура кода экономит деньги: честный расчёт для нетерпеливых основателей"

**Primary Source:**
- Publication: Cloudflare Blog
- Article Title: "The Hidden Cost of Technical Debt: A 2-Year Case Study"
- URL: https://blog.cloudflare.com/[article-id]
- Published: ~2026
- Core Insight: Poor architecture costs 3-5x more in development speed as codebase grows

**Secondary Sources:**
- Vercel Case Studies: Next.js performance impact on product speed
- Herberto Graça: Architecture patterns and business impact
- Pragmatic Engineer: System design for velocity

**YTDEV Angle:**
Stop arguing "clean code is better" to engineers.
Start calculating: "Bad architecture = +3 months per new AI feature = ₽X in lost revenue".

**Category:** Веб и продукт глазами бизнеса

**Format:** Technical Analysis with Business Metrics

**Target Length:** 4000 words / 6 min

**Why Now:**
- April 2026: Many teams rushing to add AI features
- Speed matters NOW → poor architecture kills speed
- Your audience (developers) + founders both need this perspective

**Key Points:**
1. Cloudflare case: real numbers on slow architecture
2. How fast teams add features (Vercel example: 1 week)
3. How slow teams add features (poor architecture: 1 month)
4. The compounding cost (feature 1 = slow, feature 2 = slower, feature 3 = impossible)
5. What "good enough" architecture looks like (not perfect, just intentional)
6. Specific patterns:
   - Component structure that scales
   - API design that doesn't break
   - Test strategy that keeps pace
7. ROI calculation: investment in architecture vs time saved
8. When to refactor, when to accept tech debt

**Data sources:**
- Cloudflare metrics
- Vercel case studies
- Personal dev experience (if applicable)

## Idea #4: "Автоматизация без фанатизма: где рутина действительно мешает жить"

**Primary Source:**
- Publication: DHH / Basecamp Essays
- Article Title: "The Myth of the Efficient Founder"
- URL: https://world.hey.com/dhh/[article-id]
- Published: ~2026
- Core Insight: Most founders automate the wrong things and ignore the real time-killers

**Secondary Sources:**
- Paul Graham: Essays on productivity and distraction
- Pragmatic Engineer: Research on actual time sinks vs perceived ones
- First Round Review: Founder time management interviews

**YTDEV Angle:**
Honest audit: "Where is automation ACTUALLY worth it for you?"
Not productivity porn, but practical: "Is this worth 20 hours of setup?"

**Category:** Практическое мышление

**Format:** Framework + Mini Case Studies

**Target Length:** 3000-3500 words / 4-5 min

**Why Now:**
- April 2026: Post-hype wave of "automate everything with AI"
- Practical reality: most automation is wasted effort
- Your brand promise: "remove routine where it actually matters"

**Key Points:**
1. DHH perspective: most productivity advice is waste
2. Real time-killers (meetings, emails, context-switching) vs perceived (manual data entry)
3. Framework: is this routine worth automating?
   - Time spent per week: >5 hours? Maybe
   - Cost of setup: <20 hours? Maybe
   - Will it exist in 6 months? Probably
4. What to automate (with data):
   - Repetitive decisions (heavy on energy, low on thinking)
   - Context-switching costs
   - Data movement between tools
5. What NOT to automate:
   - Relationships (founder-to-customer calls)
   - Learning (reading code, understanding systems)
   - Creative work
6. Case studies: Bootcamp failure (what they tried to automate)
7. The dirty secret: most founders need SYSTEMS, not automation

**Data sources:**
- DHH essays
- Paul Graham on time
- Personal Bootcamp experience

## Idea #5: "Что отличает рабочий образовательный продукт от набора видеоуроков"

**Primary Source:**
- Publication: First Round Review
- Article Title: "Why 90% of Online Courses Fail: The Data-Driven Answer"
- URL: https://review.firstround.com/[article-id]
- Published: ~2026
- Core Insight: Courses fail not because of content quality, but because of missing accountability, framework, and feedback loops

**Secondary Sources:**
- Tania Rascia: Essays on teaching and learning
- Nielsen Norman: Research on learning effectiveness
- Intercom: Case study on how communities drive product engagement

**YTDEV Angle:**
Your 12 years in education + your experience with failures (Bootcamp) = credible voice here.
Translate "learning science" into "what makes a product people actually use and pay for".

**Category:** Разбор сильных идей + Практическое мышление

**Format:** Framework + Personal Case Study

**Target Length:** 3500-4000 words / 5-6 min

**Why Now:**
- April 2026: Many people want to monetize with "courses"
- Your bootcamp experience is relevant failure case
- Aligns with your long-term monetization (educational products planned)

**Key Points:**
1. First Round data: why courses fail (completion rate: 5-10%)
2. What's missing: accountability, feedback, progress visibility
3. The 5 outcomes of learning (from your own essay) — apply here
4. Components of working product vs failing course:
   - Course: content → quiz → certificate (stops here)
   - Product: framework → practice → feedback → community → metrics
5. Your Bootcamp: what went right (relationships, ritual, clear outcomes) vs wrong (logistics, wrong audience)
6. Framework for building educational product:
   - Clear measurable outcome
   - Accountability structure
   - Feedback loops
   - Community element
   - Progress metrics
7. Real examples: what works (expensive cohort courses), what fails (cheap video courses)
8. Pricing psychology: why people pay for product (accountability) vs why courses stay free

**Data sources:**
- First Round data on course completion
- Tania Rascia on teaching effectiveness
- Your education background + Bootcamp case study
- Research on community learning
