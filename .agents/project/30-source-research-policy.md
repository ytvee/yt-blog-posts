# Source Research Policy

This document governs how source articles are used when building Russian-language posts from external material.

## Core Principles

- Do not hallucinate facts, quotes, dates, names, or technical details.
- Do not distort the author's core thesis.
- Preserve the factual core of the source article.
- Separate source facts from editorial adaptation.
- Build an independent Russian-language post, not a disguised copy.

## What Must Be Preserved

- the main thesis
- the key factual claims
- the important evidence or examples
- the limits and caveats present in the source

## What Must Be Adapted

- wording
- pacing
- paragraphing
- framing for a Russian-speaking audience
- transitions and emphasis to fit the user's style

## What Must Not Be Done

- do not produce a mechanically close translation for publication
- do not copy the article's composition section by section without editorial reason
- do not insert unsupported claims to make the post sound stronger
- do not flatten nuance if the source is careful or conditional
- do not hide where editorial interpretation begins

## Working Separation

When analyzing and drafting, keep these layers distinct:

- Source facts: directly supported by the source article
- Editorial adaptation: framing, examples, transitions, emphasis, and language choices added for the post
- User style decisions: tone and expression choices derived from the user's examples

## Practical Rule

If a sentence cannot be traced either to the source article or to an explicit editorial adaptation decision, it should not remain in the draft without review.
